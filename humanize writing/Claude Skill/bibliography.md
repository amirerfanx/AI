# Bibliography

The six sources this skill is built from. Citation details below are taken directly from each paper's own title page / header — no DOI or venue is listed unless it was explicitly stated in the source document.

1. Alghazo, S., Rabab'ah, G., El-Dakhs, D. A. S., & Mustafa, A. (2025). Engagement strategies in human-written and AI-generated academic essays: A corpus-based study. *Ampersand*, 15, 100237. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amper.2025.100237
   Open access, CC BY 4.0.

2. Gude, A., Santos-Ríos, R., Bond, F., Flickinger, D., Gómez-Rodríguez, C., & Zamaraeva, O. (2026). More Aligned, Less Diverse? Analyzing the Grammar and Lexicon of Two Generations of LLMs. In *Proceedings of the 64th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers)*, pp. 38900–38911. Association for Computational Linguistics.
   Code: https://github.com/olzama/llm-syntax/

3. Reinhart, A., Markey, B., Laudenbach, M., Pantusen, K., Yurko, R., Weinberg, G., & Brown, D. W. (2025). Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 122(8), e2422455122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422455122
   Distributed under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

4. O'Sullivan, J. (2025). Stylometric comparisons of human versus AI-generated creative writing. *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 12, 1708. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05986-3
   Distributed under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

5. Andoniou, C. (2026). Beyond Detection: Understanding Human–Synthetic Discursivity in the Age of AI Text Generation. *Scientific Culture*, 12(1), 216–225. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18170124
   Open access, CC BY 4.0.

6. Wegerhoff, D. (2025). Uncanny Semantics: How AI and Human Authors Use Language Differently in Academic Writing. University of Wuppertal. Working paper / preprint — no formal publication venue or DOI stated in the source document.
   Analysis code: https://github.com/DayJay1992/SemanticAIAnalysis
   Dataset: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15789515

**A note on reuse.** This skill (the SKILL.md and reference files in this repository) does not reproduce text from any of the above papers — it summarizes, paraphrases, and cites their reported findings, the same way any literature review or analysis piece does. Findings and factual claims are not subject to copyright; the specific wording of the original papers is, and none of it is copied here.

Sources 1 and 5 are published under CC BY 4.0, which permits broad reuse with attribution, including of the papers themselves. Sources 3 and 4 are published under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which restricts redistribution of the articles themselves (no derivatives, non-commercial only) — that restriction applies to republishing those papers, not to citing their findings in original work. The original PDF files are not included in this repository; only this citation list and the synthesized findings are.
